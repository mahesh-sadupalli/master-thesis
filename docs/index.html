Concurrent Neural Network Training for Compression of Spatio-Temporal Data

Interactive visualization of neural network-based concurrent compression of streaming spatio-temporal point-cloud data. Compare offline batch vs online continual learning approaches across three model sizes and architectures. Mahesh Sadupalli · BTU Cottbus-Senftenberg · M.Sc. Artificial Intelligence

This master’s thesis investigates the application of neural networks for concurrent and real-time data compression of streaming spatio-temporal point-cloud data. As modern simulations generate increasingly large data volumes due to higher resolutions and longer run-times, traditional storage and post-processing approaches face significant I/O bottlenecks and scalability limitations. This work proposes a hybrid in-situ/in-transit compression framework that employs neural networks to learn compact representations of data during runtime.

The input data consists of unstructured point-cloud coordinates with associated numeric field values, making the framework applicable to any domain that produces spatio-temporal numeric or streaming data. The methodology employs multiple neural network architectures: coordinate-based MLPs that map spatial coordinates and time directly to field variables, and Autoencoder-based models that learn compressed latent representations. Both are evaluated under two training paradigms: offline batch training on the complete dataset, and online incremental training that simulates real-time in-situ compression.

Online streaming training suffers from catastrophic forgetting, where the model progressively loses the ability to reconstruct earlier timesteps. To address this, the thesis investigates continual learning strategies based on Experience Replay (ER), evaluated in two configurations: ER Scaled (moderate replay buffer at reduced weight) and ER Aggressive (larger buffer at full replay weight). The framework is evaluated on a vortex shedding CFD dataset comprising approximately 7.9 million spatio-temporal samples across 300 timesteps (30 representative timesteps visualized here).
